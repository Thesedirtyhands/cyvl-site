The CYVL framework

An in‑depth look at how CYVL organises data through a layered architecture. Each layer plays a specific role in transforming raw inputs into insights and professional reports.

Six layers of structure

At the core of CYVL are six distinct layers, each contributing to neutrality, clarity and stability. Together they ensure that inputs are captured consistently, patterns are surfaced visually and reports remain professional.

Structured communication

Neutrality begins with the way we communicate. CYVL provides a structured environment that guides users toward civil, factual exchanges. Messages are categorised and filtered to reduce escalation, and clear prompts encourage users to stay on topic.

Guided event logging

Instead of free‑form journaling, events are recorded through predefined categories. Parents choose from options such as schedule change, pickup/drop‑off issues or medical events, ensuring consistent data that is easy to analyse later.

Schedule stability tracking

CYVL records the planned schedule and all deviations. Over time, the system creates a reliability profile, highlighting patterns of missed time or changes. This layer brings objectivity to scheduling discussions.

Pattern visualisation

Raw data is not enough. Pattern visualisation turns timelines, event frequencies and schedule reliability into graphs and charts. Visualisations reveal trends and help users and professionals understand what’s happening at a glance.

Contextual resource pop‑ups

When key events occur—such as repeated schedule changes or prolonged communication issues—CYVL offers neutral resources and guidelines. These prompts help users navigate situations calmly and stay engaged with the platform.

Courtroom artifact reporting

At the top of the stack, CYVL’s reporting layer creates neutral, professional documents. Reports emphasise patterns and timelines, not raw numbers or narratives. Each report is generated only on demand and links back to the structured data that supports it.
